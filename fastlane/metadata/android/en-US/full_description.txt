Decision Wizard turns a troubleshooting flowchart into a conversation. The app
asks one question, you tap an answer, and it asks the next one — until you reach
an endpoint that hands you the exact wording to paste into a ticket.

It was built for support agents, but it fits anything that is really a decision
tree pretending to be a wiki page: intake scripts, triage checklists, escalation
rules, returns policies.

<b>No network. No accounts. No tracking.</b> Everything happens on the device.
There is no model, no inference and no server behind this — the "bot" is a graph
being walked. The app requests no permissions at all.

<b>Chat</b>

* Walk a flow one question at a time, with wrapping answer buttons
* Each step can carry ready-made text — a ticket note, an escalation summary, a
  closing comment — with a copy button
* Tap any earlier answer to rewind and take a different branch
* Chats are named and resumable, and survive their flow being deleted
* Export a chat as plain text for the ticket
* Optional automatic clean-up of old chats

<b>Editor</b>

* Build flows on the device, on a zoomable canvas laid out automatically —
  nodes are never dragged around
* Add questions, add resolutions, connect to existing questions
* Four ways to delete: splice, delete only, delete and clean up, or reparent
* Undo and redo that say what changed and take you to it
* Long links are shown as chips at both ends; tap one to jump to the other

<b>Library</b>

* Keep several flows side by side; create, copy, import, export, delete
* Import decides for you: a newer revision offers an update, anything else
  offers a copy
* Back up every flow to a plain .zip, and restore from one

<b>Flows are files</b>

A flow is a .dwiz file, which is plain JSON you can open in any text editor,
diff, or send to a colleague to import. Nothing is locked inside the app, and a
whole-library backup is an ordinary .zip you can open on a computer.

Cycles, reconvergence and half-finished branches are all legal — the app shows
warnings, it does not refuse to save.
